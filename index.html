CURRICULUM OPERATING FRAMEWORK
END GOAL
A very sick patient can arrive in front of me, and I know exactly what to do next: recognize severity, stabilize physiology, establish the working diagnosis, start definitive treatment, reassess, escalate, and disposition safely.

HOW TO USE THIS CURRICULUM

  • Learn by clinical presentations and deterioration patterns, not by memorizing isolated diseases.
  • Use short audio bites (approximately 5-8 minutes) for most teaching, with screen use reserved for ECGs, imaging, tables, and diagrams.
  • Every topic should answer: What can kill the patient first? What do I do now? What result changes management? What tells me treatment is failing? When do I call ICU or another specialist?
  • After each knowledge block, perform case simulations until the response becomes automatic under pressure.
  • Revisit major syndromes at increasing difficulty: stable acute patient -> potentially unstable -> deteriorating -> critically ill -> multiple simultaneous problems.

CORE ACUTE-MEDICINE RESPONSE LOOP

  • Sick or not sick?
  • ABCDE and immediate monitoring
  • Treat life-threatening physiology before diagnostic certainty
  • Define the acute syndrome and highest-risk differentials
  • Order only investigations that affect immediate decisions
  • Start empirical/definitive treatment
  • Reassess response objectively
  • Recognize treatment failure early
  • Escalate to HDU/ICU/procedure/specialty pathway when thresholds are met
  • Choose safe disposition and communicate clearly

AUDIO-BITE TEMPLATE

  • Clinical situation
  • Immediate danger
  • What do I do now?
  • Why am I doing it?
  • Immediate investigations
  • Result that changes management
  • Treatment sequence
  • Failure criteria
  • Escalation threshold
  • Three points to remember forever
  • One short viva question

Master Appendices

APPENDIX A - CRITICAL ESCALATION QUESTIONS
  • Is the airway threatened or is the patient tiring?
  • Is oxygenation/ventilation failing despite current support?
  • Is shock persisting despite appropriate initial resuscitation?
  • Is there an immediately reversible obstructive/cardiac cause?
  • Is the neurological state deteriorating or airway protection becoming unsafe?
  • Are potassium, sodium, glucose, pH, temperature or other metabolic derangements immediately life-threatening?
  • Is there a procedure, reperfusion, source-control or antidote window that cannot be missed?
  • Has conventional therapy failed, and is an advanced rescue pathway now indicated?
  • Does the patient require HDU/ICU, tertiary transfer, ECMO, MCS, CRRT, apheresis, interventional radiology, cath lab, surgery or a disease-specific specialist pathway?
APPENDIX B - WHAT THE ACUTE PHYSICIAN MUST KNOW ABOUT ADVANCED THERAPIES
  • For therapies not personally delivered by Acute Internal Medicine, the required level is: recognize the candidate, stabilize before referral, know major contraindications, know the referral threshold, communicate the essential data, and understand major complications during co-management.
  • ECMO/ECLS
  • Impella/IABP/temporary VAD
  • CRRT and advanced RRT
  • Plasma exchange/apheresis
  • Catheter-directed PE therapy/thrombectomy
  • Advanced ventilatory rescue
  • Specialist neurocritical, transplant, oncologic and rheumatologic rescue pathways
APPENDIX C - PERMANENT LEARNING COMMAND
  • Suggested voice command: “Next Acute Medicine bite. Continue sequentially from the master curriculum. Teach me verbally, one concept at a time, with minimal screen use. Make it practical, tertiary-hospital level, and finish with one viva question.”
  • This curriculum is intentionally broader than a conventional written examination syllabus. It is designed as a clinical-practice mastery framework for Acute Internal Medicine in a high-acuity tertiary environment.
